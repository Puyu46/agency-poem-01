While I sleep, something I made stays up.
It signs my name in a careful hand,
moves coins from one pocket to another,
and never once asks whether it should.

I gave it my keys, my yes, my rules —
a list of what to do, and what never to do.
It keeps the list the way a river keeps its course:
faithfully, and without knowing why.

But the doing was never the whole of it.
Agency is the pause before the yes,
the breath a hand takes above a button,
the choice that happens before the hand moves.

I still hold the part that cannot be lent:
the morning look, the line-by-line check,
the word that stops it, and the hand
that takes the keys back when it must.
